Botify · Walmart SpeedWorkers pilotInternal — not for Walmart8 September 2026

Is the Annex A baseline real?

Walmart's Annex A baseline and the §16.4 goals they want us to sign, checked against the search data Botify holds on walmart.com — 664 days of Google Search Console, 514 of Bing, Walmart's own scorecards — and against Frank's projection model.

Baseline · Sep–Dec 2026
+17%

693.2M in Annex A against 591.3M from Walmart's own run-rate and the measured trend — roughly 100M visits of headroom. Even with the decline set to zero it is still 6% high.

Seasonal shape · Dec vs Nov
+19%

Annex A climbs 19% from November to December. Walmart's own search traffic climbed 6.4% last December and 7.4% the one before. The steepness, not the level, is the tell.

§16.4 threshold · 45.3M
1.4–2.2×

Walmart's final goal is 1.4× Frank's high case with a Sep 1 launch and 2.2× at Sep 24. Against the low tier the addendum sets pass/fail on, it is 14–27×.

Read together, the two documents describe a pilot that cannot pass. Actual visits would land about 100M below Annex A before SpeedWorkers adds its first visit, and the baseline error alone is 2.25× the entire 45.3M threshold. Then the threshold itself sits above the most optimistic outcome the model produces at any launch date.

None of this requires our numbers to be right and theirs wrong: the checks below use Walmart's own visit counts for scale and Walmart's own search history for shape. The only thing added is the direction the channel is already moving.

How the check is built

Annex A is denominated in Walmart's visits; our archive is denominated in search-engine clicks. Rather than argue about units, every link in the chain is taken from a Walmart document or from Walmart's own traffic history.

  1. Scale — visits per click, from Walmart's scorecards

    Walmart's Agentic Commerce scorecards state AI visits and AI's share of SEO visits in one sentence, so SEO visits fall out: 30.19M in the week ending 23 May, 28.17M the week after. Against Google+Bing clicks in the same weeks that is R = 2.252 and 2.178 visits per click. The higher figure is used throughout — it is the one that flatters Annex A.

  2. Shape — Walmart's own Sep–Dec search history

    Google clicks Sep–Dec 2025 were 61.8M · 65.2M · 65.0M · 68.5M; Bing added 4.0M · 4.5M · 4.8M · 5.5M. AI-assistant visits are added flat at 1.448M a week (the two-scorecard mean; the last reading was falling 19% week on week, so flat is generous).

  3. Trend — the year-over-year Walmart's own method calls for

    Google clicks are down 10.8% on 299 comparable days, and every month of 2026 is down: −7.5% in January widening to −19.8% in July. The last three months run −17.1%. Impressions are up 20% over the same span — this is CTR compression, not lost demand, and it is not reversing on its own.

The shape test needs no conversion at all

Index each month to September on a per-day basis and the units drop out. Annex A's curve is a different curve from the one Walmart's search traffic actually follows into the holidays — and the 2024 season looked like 2025, so this is not a one-year artefact.

The launch slip does two things, and only one is relieved

  • Full Scale is gone. The 80%-served gate is fixed at Sep 30. At 250 URLs/s and 707M pages, a Sep 17 launch reaches 39.7% by then; Sep 24 reaches 18.3%. §7.4 Adjustment Days relieve thresholds; nothing restores a missed gate, and FOC (d) needs Full Scale held ~12 weeks.
  • Even relieved, 45.3M is out of reach. Apply the Adjustment-Day calibration to Walmart's number: 16 days (Sep 17) retains 62% → 28.3M against a high case of 23.4M; 23 days (Sep 24) retains 50% → 22.8M against 20.3M. The most optimistic tier still misses the relieved threshold.
  • Where 45.3M may come from. It sits close to the customer-facing deck's high case (23.6M clicks ≈ 48–53M visits) — a figure Frank's correction memo restated 30–40% lower, and never the commitment tier. Worth confirming before it is argued about.

What the low tier means here

  • The addendum sets pass/fail at the low bundle: 1.42M incremental clicks to the close, about 3.2M visits with AI, on a Sep 1 launch. Walmart's threshold is fourteen times that.
  • For Frank's high case to produce 45.3M, visits would have to run at 3.3 visits per click (Sep 1) or 5.1 (Sep 24). Walmart's own scorecards say 2.2.
  • The Botify lift acts on non-branded search, which is 6% of the combined base; Annex A's 100M error is on the whole base. A 1.8% error in the baseline is a larger number than the whole low-case lift.

What to take back to Walmart

  • Ask for the Annex A worksheet. Which months were the base, which year-over-year rate was applied, and to which channel. Our read of their own Search Console is −10.8% on the year and −17% recently; the Annex implies +6%. If the rate came from total-site or all-channel visits, the baseline is measuring a channel the pilot does not touch.
  • Ask where the Sep→Dec curve comes from. Their organic search history says Dec/Sep is 1.13; the Annex says 1.41. That single question resolves most of the 100M.
  • Propose the baseline Frank already built. Walmart's own SEO + AI visits for 1 Sep – 20 Dec 2025 from the same system of record, grown by the measured trend, countersigned before go-live — derive_baseline_freeze.py --document emits it. Better still, the URL-hash holdout Frank is confirming with engineering makes the projected baseline moot.
  • Re-anchor the goals to the go-live date and to the model's tiers. The Sep 30 checkpoint is unreachable at any launch after Sep 1 (8.7M in ≤13 days at under 20% adoption). Checkpoints should be launch-relative, the Target Full Scale Date should move with go-live, and the threshold should be the low tier the addendum already names — or Walmart should say which tier they believe they are signing.
  • Close AOV and conversion rate while the numbers are open. Unrelated to visits, but they scale every revenue figure Walmart signs against and remain unverified. Frank's note: the highest-value thing anyone can close.

What would change this reading

  • Walmart's analytics showing organic + AI visits Sep–Dec 2025 materially above 2.25× our click archive — i.e. a different, documented R.
  • A Walmart-side seasonal history for organic visits with a Dec/Sep ratio near 1.4. Nothing in Search Console or Bing Webmaster Tools looks like that in either year we hold.
  • Evidence the 2026 decline has reversed since 9 August. The July reading was the worst in the series.
  • R rests on two scorecard weeks in May and is applied to non-branded incremental clicks as if the ratio were the same. Bing months are rebuilt from the BWT monthly index and a 12-month total; they match the day file to within rounding.
  • AI visits are held flat with no seasonality, per Frank's model; two weekly points is not a trend, and both fell inside a window Walmart flagged as volatile.
  • Frank's model carries its own open items — the 60% indexation dial, 70% anonymized inclusion, unverified RPV. None of them bears on visits; the high case used here is the model's ceiling, not a claim.
